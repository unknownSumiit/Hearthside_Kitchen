Golden Skillet Cornbread

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026

Moist buttermilk cornbread baked in a hot cast-iron skillet for crispy golden edges. Perfect with chili and stew.

Prep10 min
Cook22 min
Total32 min
Serves8
Jump to Recipe

Baking cornbread in a preheated cast-iron skillet is an old Southern technique that gives you something special: crisp, buttery, golden edges and a tender, moist center.

This recipe is lightly sweet, which suits most tastes. If you prefer a more traditional Southern cornbread, simply cut the sugar back or leave it out entirely. Serve it warm with butter and honey, alongside chili, soups, or beef stew.

Batch size
8 servings

Ingredients

Instructions

  1. Place a 10-inch cast-iron skillet in the oven and heat the oven to 425°F, so the skillet heats as the oven does.
  2. Melt 4 tablespoons of the butter and let it cool slightly.
  3. In a large bowl, whisk together the cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  4. In a separate bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs, and the 4 tablespoons of melted butter. Pour into the dry ingredients and stir just until combined.
  5. Using thick oven mitts, carefully remove the hot skillet from the oven. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons of butter and swirl until melted and coating the pan.
  6. Pour the batter into the skillet; it should sizzle. Return to the oven and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until golden brown and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean.
  7. Let cool for 5 minutes, then cut into wedges and serve warm.

Tips for success

  • Cast-iron handles get extremely hot. Keep an oven mitt draped over the handle as a reminder whenever the skillet is out of the oven.
  • No cast iron? Bake in a greased 8-inch square baking pan for 20 to 25 minutes. The edges will be softer but still delicious.

Storing and reheating

Wrap leftovers and keep at room temperature for 1 day or refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze wrapped wedges for up to 3 months. Warm in the oven or toaster oven before serving.
