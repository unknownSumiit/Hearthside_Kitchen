Classic Buttermilk Pancakes

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026

Light, fluffy buttermilk pancakes made from scratch in 30 minutes, with tips for perfect flipping and freezing extras.

🥞
Prep10 min
Cook20 min
Total30 min
Serves4
Jump to Recipe

There is something about a stack of homemade pancakes that makes any morning feel like a weekend. These classic buttermilk pancakes are tall, tender, and lightly sweet, with the slight tang that only buttermilk gives.

The secret is to stir the batter just until the flour disappears. A few small lumps are a good sign. Overmixing develops the gluten in the flour and makes pancakes tough and flat, so put the whisk down sooner than you think.

Pancakes also freeze well. Make a full batch, enjoy a few, and freeze the rest for quick breakfasts during the week.

Classic Buttermilk Pancakes

Batch size
4 servings (about 12 pancakes)

When you change the batch size, pan sizes and cooking times may need adjusting.

Ingredients

Instructions

  1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  2. In a second bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs, melted butter, and vanilla until smooth.
  3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients. Stir gently with a spatula just until no dry flour remains. The batter should be thick and a little lumpy. Let it rest for 5 minutes while the griddle heats.
  4. Heat a griddle or large nonstick skillet over medium heat (about 350°F for an electric griddle). Brush lightly with butter.
  5. Pour about 1/4 cup of batter for each pancake. Cook until bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, 2 to 3 minutes.
  6. Flip and cook until the second side is golden brown, 1 to 2 minutes more. Keep finished pancakes warm on a baking sheet in a 200°F oven while you cook the rest.

Tips for success

  • No buttermilk? Stir 2 tablespoons of lemon juice or white vinegar into 2 cups of regular milk and let it sit for 5 minutes.
  • If the first pancake browns too fast, turn the heat down slightly. The first one is always the test pancake.
  • For blueberry pancakes, pour the batter onto the griddle first, then scatter a few berries on top before flipping. This keeps the batter from turning purple.

Storing and reheating

Cool leftover pancakes completely, then freeze them in a single layer on a baking sheet. Once frozen, transfer to a zip-top freezer bag for up to 2 months. Reheat in the toaster or microwave (about 30 seconds per pancake).
