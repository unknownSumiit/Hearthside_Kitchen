Slow Cooker Beef Stew

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026

Hearty slow cooker beef stew with tender chuck roast, potatoes, carrots, and a rich gravy. About 25 minutes of prep.

🥘
Prep25 min
Cook8 hr
Total8 hr 25 min
Serves6
Jump to Recipe

There is no better feeling than walking into a house that smells of beef stew that has been cooking all day. This recipe takes about 25 minutes of work in the morning, and the slow cooker does the rest.

Beef chuck is the best cut for stew. It has enough marbling to turn meltingly tender after hours of gentle cooking, while leaner cuts tend to dry out. Browning the meat first is optional, but it adds a deeper, richer flavor that is worth the extra 10 minutes if you have them.

6 servings

Instructions

  1. In a large bowl, toss the beef cubes with the flour, salt, and pepper until evenly coated.
  2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Brown the beef in two batches, about 2 minutes per side, then transfer to the slow cooker. (You can skip browning if you are short on time, but the stew will be less rich.)
  3. Reduce the heat to medium. Add the onion to the skillet and cook for 3 to 4 minutes. Stir in the garlic and tomato paste and cook 1 minute more. Pour in 1 cup of the broth and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan.
  4. Pour the onion mixture into the slow cooker. Add the remaining broth, Worcestershire sauce, thyme, and bay leaves, then add the potatoes, carrots, and celery. Stir gently.
  5. Cover and cook on LOW for 8 hours or on HIGH for 4 to 5 hours, until the beef is fork-tender.
  6. Stir in the frozen peas and let them heat through for 10 minutes. Remove the bay leaves and season with salt and pepper to taste before serving.

Tips for success

  • For a thicker gravy, stir 2 tablespoons of cornstarch into 2 tablespoons of cold water, stir it into the stew, and cook on HIGH for 15 minutes.
  • Avoid lifting the lid during cooking. Each peek lets out heat and can add 15 to 20 minutes to the cooking time.
  • Serve with crusty bread or skillet cornbread for soaking up the gravy.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate for up to 4 days or freeze for up to 3 months. Potatoes can turn slightly grainy after freezing, but the flavor stays wonderful. Reheat on the stove until steaming hot.
