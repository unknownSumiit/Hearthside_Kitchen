Classic Meatloaf with Brown Sugar Glaze

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026

Old-fashioned meatloaf that stays moist and tender, topped with a sweet and tangy brown sugar ketchup glaze.

Prep15 min
Cook1 hr
Total1 hr 25 min
Serves6
Jump to Recipe

A good meatloaf is the definition of comfort food. This one is moist and tender inside with a shiny, sweet-and-tangy glaze on top, just like the meatloaf many of us grew up eating on Sunday nights.

Two simple tricks keep it from turning dry. First, soaking the breadcrumbs in milk creates a panade that holds moisture in the meat. Second, mix gently with your hands only until combined; overworking ground beef makes it dense.

Baking the loaf free-form on a sheet pan, rather than in a loaf pan, lets the glaze caramelize on the sides as well as the top.

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Line a rimmed baking sheet with foil for easy cleanup.
  2. In a large bowl, stir together the breadcrumbs and milk and let them soak for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the eggs, onion, garlic, ketchup, Worcestershire sauce, salt, pepper, and parsley to the bowl and stir to combine.
  4. Add the ground beef. Mix gently with your hands just until everything is evenly combined. Do not overmix.
  5. Shape the mixture into a loaf about 9 inches long and 5 inches wide on the prepared baking sheet.
  6. In a small bowl, stir together the glaze ingredients. Spread half of the glaze over the top and sides of the meatloaf.
  7. Bake for 45 minutes. Spread the remaining glaze over the top and bake 15 to 20 minutes more, until a thermometer in the center reads 160°F.
  8. Let the meatloaf rest for 10 minutes before slicing so the juices settle.

Tips for success

  • An instant-read thermometer is the most reliable way to know when meatloaf is done. Ground beef should reach 160°F.
  • To make a smaller meatloaf for two, use the Half button above. Shape it into a smaller loaf and start checking the temperature after about 40 minutes total.
  • Leftover meatloaf makes wonderful sandwiches on toasted bread with a little extra ketchup.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate leftovers for up to 4 days. Freeze individual slices, wrapped well, for up to 3 months. Reheat slices in a covered skillet with a splash of water, or in the microwave, until hot.
