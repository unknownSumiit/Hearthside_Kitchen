Handy Baking and Cooking Substitutions

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

It happens to every cook: you are halfway through a recipe and discover you are out of buttermilk, or there is no brown sugar in the pantry. Before you make a trip to the store, check this list. These substitutions are reliable and use ingredients most kitchens already have.

Dairy

If you needUse instead
1 cup buttermilk1 tablespoon lemon juice or white vinegar plus enough milk to make 1 cup. Let stand 5 minutes. Or use 3/4 cup plain yogurt thinned with 1/4 cup milk.
1 cup heavy cream (for cooking, not whipping)3/4 cup whole milk plus 1/4 cup melted butter
1 cup half-and-half1/2 cup whole milk plus 1/2 cup heavy cream, or 7/8 cup whole milk plus 1 1/2 tablespoons melted butter
1 cup sour cream1 cup plain Greek yogurt
1 cup whole milk1/2 cup evaporated milk plus 1/2 cup water

Flour, leavening, and thickeners

If you needUse instead
1 cup self-rising flour1 cup all-purpose flour plus 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder and 1/4 teaspoon salt
1 cup cake flour1 cup all-purpose flour minus 2 tablespoons, plus 2 tablespoons cornstarch. Sift together.
1 teaspoon baking powder1/4 teaspoon baking soda plus 1/2 teaspoon cream of tartar
1 tablespoon cornstarch (for thickening)2 tablespoons all-purpose flour

Note that baking soda and baking powder are not interchangeable on their own. Baking soda is about three times stronger and needs an acidic ingredient, such as buttermilk, to work.

Sugar and sweeteners

If you needUse instead
1 cup packed light brown sugar1 cup granulated sugar plus 1 tablespoon molasses
1 cup packed dark brown sugar1 cup granulated sugar plus 2 tablespoons molasses
1 cup powdered sugar1 cup granulated sugar plus 1 tablespoon cornstarch, blended in a blender until fine

Eggs

For each egg in baked goods such as muffins, quick breads, or pancakes, you can use one of these:

  • 1/4 cup unsweetened applesauce (adds moisture; best in sweet recipes)
  • 1/2 mashed ripe banana
  • 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed stirred into 3 tablespoons water, left for 5 minutes until thick

Egg substitutes work best when a recipe calls for only one or two eggs. In recipes where eggs are the star, like custards or egg muffins, there is no good substitute.

Herbs and seasonings

  • Fresh herbs to dried: use about one-third as much dried herb. For example, 1 tablespoon of fresh dill equals about 1 teaspoon of dried dill.
  • 1 clove garlic: 1/8 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 small onion: 1 tablespoon dried minced onion or 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning: 1/4 teaspoon each of dried oregano, basil, thyme, and rosemary
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice: 1/2 teaspoon white vinegar (in cooking, not as a garnish)

Pans

A 9-inch square pan and an 11x7-inch pan hold about the same amount, so they can usually be swapped. A 9x13-inch pan holds roughly as much as two 8-inch square pans. When you change pan sizes, keep an eye on baking times. A shallower layer bakes faster, and a deeper one takes longer.
