10 Kitchen Tools That Make Cooking Easier

By the Hearthside Kitchen team · Updated November 13, 2026 · 6 min read

You do not need a kitchen full of gadgets to cook wonderful meals. But a few well-chosen tools can make everyday cooking easier, safer, and more comfortable on the hands and back. Here are ten that experienced home cooks rely on, most of them inexpensive and widely available.

1. An instant-read thermometer

If you buy only one tool from this list, make it this one. A digital instant-read thermometer takes the guesswork out of cooking meat, poultry, and fish, and it is the best way to know food is cooked safely. Chicken should reach 165°F, ground beef 160°F, and fish 145°F. Look for one with a large, easy-to-read display.

2. A good chef’s knife and a honing steel

It sounds surprising, but a sharp knife is safer than a dull one. A dull blade needs more force and is more likely to slip. An 8-inch chef’s knife handles almost every job. Consider one with a comfortable, non-slip handle, and have it professionally sharpened once or twice a year.

3. A non-slip cutting board

A cutting board that slides around the counter is an accident waiting to happen. Choose one with rubber feet or edges, or place a damp paper towel or a piece of shelf liner underneath to hold it steady.

4. A jar opener

Stubborn jar lids frustrate everyone. A simple rubber grip pad or an under-cabinet jar opener makes them easy to open with very little hand strength.

5. A large-print measuring set

Many measuring cups and spoons have markings that are tiny and fade over time. Sets with large, engraved, or color-coded markings are much easier to read. Magnetic measuring spoons that stack together are also less likely to get lost in the drawer.

6. A sheet pan (or two)

A sturdy rimmed half-sheet pan is one of the most versatile pieces of equipment in the kitchen. Use it for roasting vegetables, baking cookies, and complete one-pan dinners like our sheet pan lemon herb chicken. Lining it with parchment or foil means almost no scrubbing afterward.

7. A slow cooker

A slow cooker lets you prepare dinner in the morning, when you have the most energy, and come home to a finished meal. It is ideal for tough, inexpensive cuts of meat that turn tender with long cooking, as in our slow cooker beef stew. A small 3-quart model is plenty for one or two people.

8. An immersion blender

This handheld stick blender purées soups right in the pot, so there is no need to pour hot liquid into a countertop blender. It is perfect for creamy tomato soup and much easier to clean than a full blender.

9. A kitchen timer you can hear

Your phone works well as a kitchen timer, and you can even ask Siri to set one hands-free. If you prefer a separate timer, choose one with a loud alarm and large numbers. A timer you can clip to your shirt goes with you if you step out of the kitchen.

10. Long oven mitts

Thin potholders and short mitts leave wrists exposed to hot oven racks. Silicone or heavy cotton mitts that reach up the forearm offer much better protection, especially when lifting a heavy cast-iron skillet or a casserole dish.

Small changes that help

  • Keep the tools you use every day within easy reach, between hip and shoulder height, to avoid bending and stretching.
  • Use a stool or a high chair to sit while you chop and prepare ingredients.
  • Add brighter under-cabinet lighting to make labels, measurements, and knives easier to see.
  • Choose lighter-weight pots and pans for everyday cooking and save the heavy cast iron for special jobs.

With the right tools in place, cooking at home becomes more relaxing and more enjoyable. Ready to put them to use? Browse all our recipes.
